What is construction petty cash management?

Construction petty cash management is how a contractor controls small cash spends on site. Head office gives a site engineer or supervisor a fixed float (an imprest). The custodian records each expense with a bill, and head office tops the float back up by the amount spent. At any time you know what was issued, spent, returned and still in hand.

The government's own accounts code describes an imprest as "a standing advance of a fixed sum of money given to an individual to enable him to make certain classes of disbursements" (CPWA Code). A site float works the same way.

Where site cash leaks

  • Spends without bills. "Paid ₹2,000 for transport" with nothing to show for it.
  • Advances that never settle. Cash given to an engineer three months ago, still "in hand".
  • Mixed with personal money. One pocket, two purposes.
  • Bills on WhatsApp. Photos sent to a group, never matched to a ledger, lost when the phone changes.
  • Big payments in cash. Payments that should have gone by UPI or bank, and now cause tax trouble.

Petty cash rules every Indian contractor should know

Cash payment limit. Under the cash-payment rule in Section 40A(3) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, a business expense paid in cash above ₹10,000 to one person in a day is disallowed (₹35,000 for payments to goods transporters), and splitting the payment doesn't get around it (TaxGuru explainer). The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, so confirm the current section reference with your CA.

  • Keep contractor payments out of petty cash. Payments to contractors can attract TDS (Section 393, formerly 194C). Pay them through billing, not from a site float. See subcontractor billing.
  • Get a tax invoice for GST credit. To claim input tax credit, you generally need a proper tax invoice with your firm's GSTIN. A cash memo won't do.
  • Labour wages belong in the labour register. Regular wages should run through attendance and payroll. See labour attendance.

A simple site petty cash policy

  1. One named custodian per site, with a fixed float set by head office.
  2. Every spend logged the same day, with a photo of the bill.
  3. No single cash payment above ₹10,000 to one person in a day. Use UPI or bank.
  4. No contractor payments, salaries or personal spends from the float.
  5. Top-ups only against logged expenses.
  6. Any advance not settled within 45 days is escalated to the project manager.

Frequently asked questions

What is the imprest system in construction?

Head office gives a site custodian a fixed float. The custodian spends it on approved small expenses with bills, and head office tops it up by exactly the amount spent, so the float always returns to the same figure.

How much petty cash should a construction site keep?

Enough for one to two weeks of genuine small spends at that site. Start with a fixed float, watch how fast it's used, and adjust. A float that is never fully used is too big.

Can I pay more than ₹10,000 in cash from petty cash?

Avoid it. Under the cash-payment rule (Section 40A(3) of the 1961 Act), a cash expense above ₹10,000 to one person in a day is disallowed, with a ₹35,000 limit for goods transporters. Pay larger amounts by UPI or bank.

How do you reconcile site petty cash?

Compare cash issued with expenses logged, cash returned and cash in hand for each custodian. In True Site Sync, the reconciliation screen shows these figures for each custodian and flags gaps.

What should petty cash not be used for?

Contractor and subcontractor payments (which can attract TDS), regular salaries and wages, large supplier payments and personal spends.

Does it work without internet on site?

Yes. Expenses and bill photos save on the phone with no signal and sync to head office when the connection returns.

Who should approve petty cash top-ups?

The project manager or accounts, based on the logged expenses and bills since the last top-up, never on a request for a round figure.
