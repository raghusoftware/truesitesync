Five steps to a schedule the site will follow

1. Break the job into locations

Plan by block, floor or zone, not just by activity.

2. Set durations from output

Duration = quantity ÷ (crew size × output per man-day). See the productivity calculator.

3. Link the tasks

Use finish-to-start links. The longest chain of linked tasks is your critical path.

4. Run a 3-week look-ahead

Every week, list the next three weeks' tasks and clear their constraints: drawings, material, labour, approvals.

5. Plan labour daily

Allocate gangs to tasks each day and compare with what got done.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a construction schedule?

Split the job into locations, list activities in their construction sequence, set each duration from quantity and crew output, link tasks to the ones they wait for, and draw it as a Gantt chart. Review it weekly with a 3-week look-ahead.

What is a Gantt chart in construction?

A bar chart with activities down the side and dates across the top. Each bar shows when a task starts and finishes, and links show which tasks wait for others.

What is the sequence of building construction?

Layout, excavation, PCC, footings, plinth, backfilling, then columns, beams and slabs floor by floor, followed by masonry, MEP conduits, plaster, flooring, doors and windows, painting and handover.

What is a look-ahead schedule?

A short-range plan, usually 3 weeks, taken from the master schedule. It lists the tasks coming up and the constraints to clear first: drawings, material, labour and approvals.

How do I calculate activity duration?

Duration in days = quantity ÷ (number of workers × output per man-day). For example, 600 m² of plaster ÷ (10 × 15) = 4 days.
