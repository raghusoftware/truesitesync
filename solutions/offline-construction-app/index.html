Why offline is non-negotiable on real sites

Highways, metros, tunnels, dams and greenfield projects are built exactly where cellular coverage is worst. Cloud-only construction apps assume a constant connection — so on these sites they spin, time out, or silently drop entries, and crews give up and go back to paper, WhatsApp and end-of-day Excel. An offline-first app flips that: every entry is written to the device the instant it is made, so logging a DPR or a measurement never depends on signal. Sync happens quietly in the background whenever the phone finds data, so the office still gets everything — just slightly later, never lost.

Offline-first, not offline-only: the field works with no signal; the office sees the same records once the device syncs — one shared workspace, no manual export.

The "two-tap" test — why field crews actually use it

Software only helps if the people at the workface use it, and aging crews with calloused hands will abandon anything that buries a simple log under enterprise menus. True Site Sync is designed to pass the two-tap test: the most common daily actions — mark attendance, add a measurement, log material — are reachable in about two taps, work offline, and don't drain the battery. That is the difference between a tool that lives on site and one that quietly dies after week one.
