## 3. Snag categories, practical checklist for Indian handover, RERA Section 14(3)

### Takeaway
Indian guides converge on room-by-room categories: walls/ceilings (cracks, damp, seepage, uneven paint), floors/tiles (hollow tiles, chips, grout), doors/windows (alignment, closing, hardware), plumbing (leaks, drainage, pressure), electrical (every socket/switch tested), kitchen/bathroom, and finishes/fittings versus the agreement. RERA Section 14(3) is confirmed via several secondary sources: defects in structure, workmanship, quality or services, reported within 5 years of handing over possession, must be rectified by the promoter without further charge within 30 days; failing that the allottee can claim compensation. Note it covers more than "structural" defects.

### Cited Findings
**Categories / checks (sources are guides; summarised by search tool):**
- Walls and ceilings: cracks, damp patches, uneven paint, seepage — [StudioMatrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/snagging-punch-list-handover-india)
- Checklist covers walls, floors, doors, windows, plumbing, drainage, electrical points, carpet area, and whether fittings match the agreement — [RealtyHunting](https://realtyhunting.com/flat-inspection-before-possession)
- Seepage, hollow tiles, doors that won't close are flagged as things to note — [PropNewz](https://www.propnewz.com/blog/taking-possession-snag-list-handover-bengaluru-flat-buyer-2026-09-28)
- Test taps, switches, doors; run water to check drainage; photograph each defect — [StudioMatrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/snagging-punch-list-handover-india)
- Tools to bring: torch, a charger for testing sockets, phone for photos — [RealtyHunting](https://realtyhunting.com/flat-possession-checklist)
- Generic examples: paint blemishes, scratches on tiles/counters, missing grout, paint bubbles — [GoCodes](https://gocodes.com/snagging-in-construction/); faulty sockets and leaking pipes treated as serious — [Bayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/property-snagging/); structural cracks, out-of-plumb walls, code non-compliance as major — [Deltek](https://www.deltek.com/en-au/architecture-and-engineering/archisnapper/snag-list)
- Bathroom-specific handover checklist exists as a separate India page (topic signal for waterproofing/plumbing depth) — [StudioMatrx bathroom](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/bathroom-handover-checklist-india)
- KRERA ordered a developer to repair recurring seepage in a Bengaluru flat under the defect liability provision — [The Realty Today](https://therealtytoday.com/news/regulatory/krera-directs-developer-to-repair-seepage-in-bengaluru-flat-after-buyer-complaint/)

**RERA Section 14(3):**
- Section 14(3), Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016: the promoter must rectify any structural defect or any other defect in workmanship, quality or provision of services, or other obligations under the agreement, brought to its notice by the allottee within five years from the date of handing over possession, without further charge, within thirty days; on failure, aggrieved allottees are entitled to appropriate compensation as provided under the Act — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/defect-liability-period-rera.html), [Law Insider clause samples](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/defect-liability/_2), [MahaRERA order PDF via LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/pdf_upload/maharashtra-rera-542896.pdf) (wording via search-tool summary; primary text at India Code not reachable)
- "Structural defects" is not defined in the RERA Act; some states define it in their rules; normal wear and tear is usually excluded — [Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/defect-liability-period-what-every-homebuyer-must-know/) / [Housiey](https://housiey.com/blogs/?p=10840) (search-tool summary)
- MahaRERA rejected a Borivali developer's argument that liability was only three years and directed structural defects be fixed within 30 days — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/maharera-directs-borivali-developer-to-fix-structural-defects-in-flats-within-30-days)
- Some sale agreements count the five years from the completion certificate rather than handover, or try to limit liability to structural defects or exclude allottee-caused defects — [Law Insider](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/defect-liability/_2)
- Secondary sources differ slightly on the remedy wording (compensation "as decided by the state RERA" vs "under the Act") — [Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/compensation-for-defects-in-construction-after-possession-under-rera/), [Housing.com builder warranty](https://housing.com/news/what-is-builder-warranty-under-rera)

### Inferences
- The brief's framing "structural defects reported within 5 years must be rectified within 30 days" is correct but incomplete: 14(3) also covers workmanship, quality and services defects. The page should say "structural defects and defects in workmanship, quality or services", run from handing over possession, rectified within 30 days, without charge. Recommend a legal review line and linking to the India Code text.
- How snag lists relate: the pre-handover snag list closes defects before the clock starts; after possession, every buyer complaint is effectively a snag with a 30-day statutory due date. A tool with due dates, Delayed status and photo proof of closure creates the evidence trail a builder needs if a complaint reaches RERA (inference, not legal advice).
- Suggested checklist sections for the page (built from the categories above plus standard Indian site practice; mark the practice items as guidance, not sourced): Civil and structure (cracks, honeycombing, plumb); Plaster and paint (hairline cracks, undulations, patches, shade mismatch); Flooring and tiling (hollow tiles by tap test, lippage, grout, chips, slope to drain); Waterproofing and dampness (bathroom/terrace/balcony ponding signs, seepage at skirting and ceilings); Doors and windows (alignment, closing, locks, hardware, sealant, glass); Electrical (every socket and switch, MCB labelling, earthing, fan/light points); Plumbing and sanitary (leaks, water pressure, drainage, CP fittings, traps); Kitchen (platform, sink, exhaust point); Fixtures and specs vs agreement; Common areas (lift, staircase, parking) for builders.

### Gaps
- No primary-source (India Code / gazette) confirmation of exact 14(3) wording due to blocked fetch.
- No Indian standard (IS code / CPWD) tolerances were retrieved for tiles, plaster or waterproofing; any numeric tolerances on the page need separate sourcing.
