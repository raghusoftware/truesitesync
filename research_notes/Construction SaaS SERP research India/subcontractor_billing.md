## 5. Current law: TDS on contractors (194C → Section 393) and GST on works contracts

### Takeaway
- **Section 194C (1961 Act) to Section 393 (2025 Act).** The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026 (PIB). The contractor TDS provision now sits in Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 6(i). The rates are unchanged: 1% for individual/HUF and 2% for others, and 20% without PAN. The thresholds are unchanged: ₹30,000 for a single payment and ₹1,00,000 aggregate in the year. Budget 2025 did **not** change the 194C thresholds.
- **GST on works contracts.** GST 2.0 (from 22 Sept 2025) moved most works contracts, including the earlier 12% government works entry, to 18%. Sources conflict on whether narrow concessional entries survive (e.g., earthwork-dominant government works). Verify against the CBIC rate notification.

### Cited Findings
**Income-tax Act 2025 commencement**
- PIB: "Income-tax Act, 2025 comes into force from today (1st April, 2026)" — [PIB PRID 2248005](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2248005&reg=48&lang=2); earlier PIB note that it takes effect from 1 April 2026 — [PIB PRID 2221416](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2221416&reg=48&lang=2)
- Official consolidated text of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (as amended by Finance Act, 2026) — [incometaxindia.gov.in PDF](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf); section 1 — [incometaxindia.gov.in](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-1-1)
- PIB says the Act is designed not to change tax policy or rates. It replaces "previous year"/"assessment year" with "tax year". CBDT notified the Income-tax Rules, 2026 on 20 March 2026 (per search summary of the PIB release) — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2248005&reg=48&lang=2), [PIB explainer](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?ModuleId=3&NoteId=155137&reg=3&lang=2)

**Section 393 contractor entry**
- The official Section 393 page sets the table-based structure: income or sum in column B, paid by the person in column C, deducted at the rate and threshold in column D — [incometaxindia.gov.in section-393](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-393-6) (page fetch blocked; seen via search extract only)
- Contractor payments ("carrying out any work (including supply of labour…) in pursuance of a contract") sit at Sl. No. 6(i). D(a) covers individual/HUF at 1% and D(b) covers others at 2%. Thresholds are ₹30,000 single and ₹1,00,000 aggregate. Effective 1 April 2026 — [TDSMan](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/06/tds-on-payments-to-contractors-section-3931-section-194c/), [Busy](https://busy.in/tds/section-194c-contractors-subcontractors/), [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/tag/tds-on-contractor-new-income-tax-act-2025), [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_notes?id=1858)
- Payment/return codes 1023 (individual/HUF contractor) and 1024 (other contractors) are cited by secondary sources — [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=120666) (secondary, not verified officially)
- Busy says non-salary TDS returns for FY 2026-27 move to Form 140 (formerly 26Q) and certificates to Form 131 (formerly 16A). Other guides still say 26Q, so **this is unconfirmed** — [Busy](https://busy.in/tds/section-194c-contractors-subcontractors/)

**Rates and thresholds (1961 Act 194C, carried over)**
- 1% for individual/HUF and 2% for others. No TDS if each payment is ≤ ₹30,000 **and** the FY aggregate is ≤ ₹1,00,000. Once a limit is crossed, TDS applies to the whole amount (e.g., the entire ₹1,20,000, not only the excess). The rate is 20% without PAN (s. 206AA). Deduction happens at the earlier of credit or payment — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-194c-tds-on-contractor), [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/section-194c-tds-payments-contractors-sub-contractors.html)
- Budget 2025 (effective 1 Apr 2025) raised thresholds for 194A, 194H (₹15k → ₹20k), 194J (₹30k → ₹50k), 194I and 194LA. It did **not** list 194C — [Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/trilegal-update-budget-2025-rationalisation-of-tds-and-tcs-provisions/), [TDSMan Budget 2025](https://blog.tdsman.com/2025/02/key-tds-tcs-proposed-changes-in-budget-2025-26/), [TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/tds-under-194h-and-194c-explore-differences-key-changes-and-implications-for-2025)
- TDS is on the amount excluding GST where GST is shown separately (CBDT Circular 23/2017) — [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/tds-on-gst.html)

**GST on works contracts**
- GST 2.0 took effect on 22 Sept 2025. The 12% slab was largely eliminated and works contracts generally moved to 18% — [Disytax](https://disytax.com/?p=24147), [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-on-works-contract-construction-services-india-2026)
- The earlier concessional 12% for government works contracts and their sub-contractors was withdrawn by Notification 09/2025-CT(R), per one guide. Supplies on or after 22 Sept 2025 attract 18% with ITC, and existing contracts need transition analysis — [TaxClue](https://taxclue.in/guide/gst-on-works-contract)
- **Conflict.** TaxAj says concessional 12% still applies to some specified government infrastructure works and offshore oil & gas works contracts, and warns not to apply it merely because the customer is a government entity — [TaxAj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/gst-on-works-contract-and-construction-services-rate-itc-and-rcm-2026/). TaxGarden says all works contracts are at 18% — [TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-on-works-contract-construction-services-india-2026). A table lists earthwork-dominant government works as 12% before 22.09.2025 and 18% after — [MS Associates](https://www.msassociates.pro/gst/gst-service-construction.php)
- GST TDS under Section 51 (government/PSU deductors) is 2% (1% CGST + 1% SGST, or 2% IGST), filed via GSTR-7. Threshold is commonly ₹2.5 lakh contract value — [GSTZen](https://gstzen.in/a/tax-deducted-at-source.html), [Teachoo](https://www.teachoo.com/9399/1969/TDS-in-GST-(Dec-2018)/category/TDS-in-GST/)

### Inferences

No Indian page combines a **worked, numeric subcontractor RA bill** (gross, previous, current, retention, advance recovery, material and debit-note deductions, GST, TDS on the ex-GST value, net payable) with **up-to-date law** (Section 393 / GST 2.0), **back-to-back linkage to the client RA bill**, and **labour-contractor/thekedar and MEP specifics**. That combination is the opportunity.
