# Regulatory and tax changes in India (2025–2026) creating rising search demand from construction contractors

Research date: 15 Oct 2026. Method: web search (the tool is US-located; pib.gov.in and housing.com could not be fetched directly from this environment, so official text was confirmed via search snippets of the PIB release, not a full read). No keyword-volume or Google Trends data was available. "Rising interest" below is inferred from proxies: how many CA, tax-software and vendor articles appeared, and how recently. Treat every rate as "verify against the CBIC/Gazette notification before publishing".

## Q1. GST 2.0 (rate rationalisation, effective 22 Sep 2025): what changed for construction, and how contractors re-price

### Takeaway
Cement went from 28% to 18% and sand-lime bricks and marble/granite blocks went from 12% to 5%, all from 22 Sep 2025. Most works contracts stayed at 18%. One works contract category went UP: composite works contracts to Government that are predominantly earthwork (>75%), and the subcontracts under them, moved from 12% to 18%. That increase, plus rate changes landing on running contracts, gives contractors a re-pricing and RA-bill problem. Generic "GST on cement" content is already crowded.

### Cited Findings
- 56th GST Council recommendations (PIB release): sand-lime bricks and marble/travertine/granite blocks cut from 12% to 5%. Composite works contracts for predominantly earthwork (>75% of value) supplied to Government rise from 12% to 18% with ITC, and so do subcontractors supplying such works to the main contractor — [PIB, 56th GST Council](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555&reg=48&lang=2) (seen via search snippet; full page not fetchable here); same in [CNK Newsflash 3 Sep 2025](https://cnkindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/CNK-Newsflash-56th-GST-Council-Meeting.pdf). CNK also lists offshore oil & gas works contracts going from 12% to 18%.
- Cement (HSN 2523) is now 18%, down from 28%, effective 22 Sep 2025 by CBIC notification — [Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/cement-gst-percentage-rate-hsn-code-applicability/); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/tax/gst-rates-on-construction-materials-in-2025-gst-on-cement-sand-and-bricks).
- Marble/granite block cut from 12% to 5% confirmed by the Ministry of Mines statement — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/gst-reforms-in-mining-sector-set-to-boost-housing-and-small-scale-enterprises-125091100676_1.html).
- Most works contract and construction services remain at 18%. The 1% affordable-housing rate (no ITC) is unchanged — [Disytax](https://disytax.com/?p=24147). Sources conflict on residential rates (5%/18%) and some tables say "expected" rates, so do not quote them without the notification — [busy.in works contract](https://busy.in/gst-rates/work-contract).
- Housewise's 2026 article still lists marble blocks at 12%, which is outdated against PIB. This is an example of stale competitor content — [Housewise](https://housewise.in/blog/gst-on-cement-impact-on-house-construction/).
- Re-pricing mechanics: the applicable rate on a rate change follows time of supply (CGST s.14: supply, invoice and payment dates). Section 64A of the Sale of Goods Act allows price adjustment for tax changes unless the contract shows a different intention, such as "inclusive of GST" wording — [Tally, rate reclassification](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-rate-reclassification/); [Taxclue, change-in-law clause](https://taxclue.in/blog/gst-rate-change-contracts-change-in-law-clause-repricing).
- Advisory commentary recommends that contractors invoice promptly on milestone completion to lock in a rate, and include a "Change in Law"/"Tax Variation" clause — [Taxheal, s.14 & rate change](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=115227).
- Precedent from the 2022 hike (12% to 18% on government works, Notification 03/2022): West Bengal allowed the unbilled value to be raised up to 6% where 12% GST was stated in the tender, and not where no rate was stated — [wbxpress memo](https://wbxpress.com/change-contract-amount-enhancement-gst/). Madras HC directed NHAI to consider paying bills at 18% — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=257531). Madras HC also held that contractors "cannot be left high and dry" on unforeseen rate revisions — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/rss_feed_blog?id=813984).
- Competition: cement makers (JK Cement: [1](https://www.jkcement.com/gst-2-0-home-construction-guide-2025/), [2](https://www.jkcement.com/construction-materials-gst-2025-guide/)), housing.com, Outlook Money, Tally, busy.in and Disytax all have "GST 2.0 construction materials" pages.

### Inferences
- "GST on cement 2025/2026" and "new GST rates construction materials" are crowded by authoritative and brand domains. True Site Sync (TSS) is unlikely to win these head terms.
- Less contested and a better fit: (a) "GST rate change on ongoing works contracts / RA bills: which rate applies" (time of supply applied to running-account bills, with worked examples from before and after 22 Sep 2025); (b) "GST on earthwork contracts for government, 12% to 18%" plus the subcontractor angle; (c) "price variation / change-in-law clause for GST rate change", with a template. All three map directly to TSS RA billing and GST invoicing.
- The existing TSS blog that claims 12% is now factually wrong for most works contracts, and for government earthwork after Sep 2025. Updating it is urgent for credibility (E-E-A-T).

### Gaps
- Could not read the CBIC rate notification (reported as No. 9/2025-CT(Rate)) or the full PIB page; exact entry wording is unverified.
- No search-volume data (Google Trends or Keyword Planner) for "GST 2.0 works contract" and similar terms.
- No official clarification found on how GST 2.0 applies to RA bills raised across 22 Sep 2025.

## Q2. Section 43B(h) (MSME 45-day payment) and its status under the Income-tax Act 2025

### Takeaway
The rule carries forward. Per a Rajya Sabha reply, s.43B(h) corresponds to s.37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act 2025 (in force 1 Apr 2026). A buyer gets a deduction for a payment owed to a micro or small enterprise only when it is paid, if it is paid beyond the MSMED Act s.15 limit (15 days without a written agreement, 45 days maximum with one). This matters for contractors that buy from MSME material suppliers and MSME-registered subcontractors.

### Cited Findings
- Rajya Sabha reply (21 Jul 2026, as quoted): s.43B(h) of the 1961 Act corresponds to s.37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act 2025 — reported in search results drawing on [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1060588) and [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/section-43bh-msme-payment-rule). (I did not read the reply itself; verify.)
- Payment window: 15 days with no written agreement, up to 45 days with one, counted from acceptance or deemed acceptance — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-43bh-of-income-tax-act); [busy.in](https://busy.in/tds/section-43bh-msme-payment-rule-and-45-day-limit-explained/).
- Effective-year confusion in secondary sources (AY 2024-25 vs FY 2024-25) — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-43bh-of-income-tax-act) vs [L&T Finance](https://www.ltfinance.com/blog/business-loan/msme-45-days-payment-rule).
- Some blogs claim the rule does not apply to traders. Doubtful against the statutory wording "any sum payable to a micro or small enterprise". Treat as contested — [Setindiabiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/45-day-msme-payment-rule).

### Inferences
- "Section 37(2)(g)" is a new identifier from 1 Apr 2026, with few pages yet. Searches like "43B(h) new section number" and "37(2)(g) income tax act 2025" are likely rising as FY 2026-27 audits and tax-audit reports start using the new numbering.
- Construction angle with little competition: "MSME 45-day rule for contractors: subcontractor RA bills, retention money and certification delays". Key questions: when is a running bill "accepted", and does retention held beyond 45 days trigger disallowance? Fits TSS RA bill payment timeline, retention tracking and subcontractor billing. Link to the existing pages rather than duplicating them.

### Gaps
- Did not read the Rajya Sabha reply or the text of s.37(2)(g); exact wording and any changes from 43B(h) unverified.
- No authoritative ruling found on retention money or RA-bill certification under 43B(h); this is a known grey area. Present it as a question, not as settled law.

## Q3. Income-tax Act 2025 (effective 1 Apr 2026): contractor-relevant renumbering

### Takeaway
Contractor TDS (old s.194C) now sits in s.393(1), Table Sl. No. 6(i). Rates and thresholds are substantively unchanged (1% individual/HUF, 2% others; ₹30,000 per contract and ₹1,00,000 aggregate). The searchable novelty is the new section and table references, not new rates.

### Cited Findings
- 194C maps to s.393(1) [Table Sl. No. 6(i)] from 1 Apr 2026, with no substantive change beyond renumbering — [busy.in](https://busy.in/tds/section-194c-contractors-subcontractors/); [Taxheal](https://www.taxheal.com/tds-on-contractors-w-e-f-01-4-2026.html).
- Rates 1%/2%; thresholds ₹30,000 single / ₹1,00,000 aggregate; higher rate without PAN — [Taxheal](https://www.taxheal.com/tds-on-contractors-w-e-f-01-4-2026.html); [Incorpx](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/tds-under-income-tax-act-2025).
- General "TDS under Income Tax Act 2025" explainers exist — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1044146); [TaxTMI notes](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_notes?id=1858).

### Inferences
- "Section 393 TDS on contractor" and "194C new section 2026" are likely rising, with mostly tax-site competition (busy.in, Taxheal, TaxGuru).
- TSS already has a 194C blog. Recommendation: update it with a "194C is now s.393(1) Table 6(i)" section and old-to-new mapping, rather than creating a new page (avoids cannibalisation).

### Gaps
- New TDS return and certificate form numbers under the Income-tax Rules 2026 (successors to Form 26Q/16A) were not verified.
- The Act/CBDT table text was not read directly.

## Q4. E-invoicing: 30-day IRP limit (≥₹10 cr AATO, from 1 Apr 2025) and any 2026 changes

### Takeaway
GSTN advisory of 5 Nov 2024: from 1 Apr 2025, taxpayers with AATO of ₹10 crore or more cannot report an invoice, credit note or debit note to the IRP more than 30 days after its date. The IRP rejects the upload and no IRN is generated. I found no 2026 change. E-invoicing itself still applies above ₹5 crore AATO.

### Cited Findings
- GSTN advisory lowering the 30-day threshold from ₹100 cr to ₹10 cr — [GST Gyaan (advisory text)](https://www.gstgyaan.com/advisory-time-limit-for-reporting-e-invoice-on-the-irp-portal-lowering-of-threshold-to-aato-10-cr-and-above); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/news?id=28072).
- A late upload is rejected, no IRN is issued, and the buyer's ITC is affected — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/accounting/30-day-e-invoice-reporting-limit-impact-on-itc-buyers-and-sellers/).
- Businesses between ₹5 cr and ₹10 cr must e-invoice but are not subject to the 30-day block — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1089104).

### Inferences
- This is now about 18 months old, so it is a steady topic rather than a breakout. Construction angle: an RA bill certified late by the client while the contractor's invoice is dated at the measurement date can blow the 30-day window. "E-invoice for RA bills / running bills" is an under-served long-tail topic that fits TSS GST invoicing.

### Gaps
- No 2026 advisory found that changes thresholds (e.g. a lower AATO or a shorter window); confirm on einvoice.gst.gov.in.

## Q5. Labour Codes (in force 21 Nov 2025; central rules May 2026): what contractors need

### Takeaway
The four Codes are in force from 21 Nov 2025 and the CLRA has been repealed. Central OSH Rules were notified 8 May 2026 (G.S.R. 345(E)). Contractor licensing moves from annual, establishment-specific licences to a single licence (reported as 5 years, pan-India or multi-state, via Shram Suvidha). The threshold is a uniform 50+ contract workers. The "wages" definition adds back allowances above 50% of remuneration, which affects PF, ESI, gratuity and bonus. State rules are uneven, and the Central Rules bind only establishments where the Centre is the appropriate government.

### Cited Findings
- Codes effective 21 Nov 2025 (state-wise trackers) — [LKS state pages, e.g. Assam](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/states/assam); [LKS "what changed 2025"](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/what-changed-2025).
- 50% wage add-back rule; review pay structures and PF/ESI; affects gratuity and bonus — [LKS Assam](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/states/assam); [LKS Sikkim](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/states/sikkim).
- Fixed-term employment with parity benefits and pro-rata gratuity — [LKS Assam](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/states/assam).
- CLRA repealed on OSH Code commencement. Central OSH Rules G.S.R. 345(E) of 8 May 2026. Single contractor licence valid 5 years across states/India via Form XXI on Shram Suvidha. Uniform 50+ contract-worker threshold (s.45) — [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/major-compliance-related-changes-under-the-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-code-2020-state-rules-are-at-different-stages-of-finalisation/); [factoHR](https://factohr.com/occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-code/); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/?p=130133).
- Central rules notified 8 and 9 May 2026 (consultancy notice) — [MBG Corp](https://www.mbgcorp.com/in/insights/ministry-of-labour-employment-notifies-final-central-rules-under-labour-codes-model-standing-orders-2026/).
- Contractor vs principal employer duties: the contractor pays wages and the principal employer is liable on default. Courts draw adverse inferences from missing or inconsistent registers. The contractor must electronically inform the principal employer of wages paid — [Corporate Professionals, 6 May 2026](https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/articles/contractor-principal-employer-duties-obligations-and-vicarious-liabilities-under-labour-codes/) (partly based on draft rules).
- Some states (e.g. Delhi) had only draft rules at the time of the tracker — [LKS Delhi](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/states/delhi).

### Inferences
- High-growth cluster with construction-specific long tail: "contractor licence under OSH Code", "labour code registers for contractors", "wages definition 50% rule PF calculation for contract labour", "principal employer liability labour code". Competition is law firms and HR SaaS (factoHR, Keka), with little construction-specific content.
- Strong fit with TSS labour attendance and wage records: "digital attendance and wage registers that satisfy the Labour Codes" is a credible ownable angle. Keep legal claims hedged and state-specific.

### Gaps
- The 5-year licence and 50-worker threshold come from secondary sources; G.S.R. 345(E) was not read. State-by-state status as of Oct 2026 not verified.
- Final register formats (unified registers) under the central rules were not retrieved.

## Q6. Other 2026 changes: BOCW cess, e-way bill, IMS, RERA, TDS on GST

### Takeaway
- The BOCW cess now sits under the Social Security Code. A notification dated 8 May 2026 sets it at 1% of construction cost, with self-assessment and final assessment within 60 days of completion.
- The e-way bill Ship-To GSTIN mandate proposed in May 2026 was put on hold on 29 Jul 2026. 2FA, the 180-day document-age limit and the 360-day extension cap continue to apply.
- IMS is widely reported as mandatory from 1 Apr 2026, but I could not verify this officially.
- RERA: the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act 2026 decriminalised some offences (reported effective 7 May 2026). The "RERA 2.0" claims are poorly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- BOCW cess under Code on Social Security s.100(1): band 1–2%, fixed at 1% by notification dated 8 May 2026 (replacing the 1996 notification). Excludes land cost. Advance payment on chartered-engineer-certified self-assessment, final assessment within 60 days of completing each work — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-bocw-cess-under-code-on-social-security/); [Taxheal](https://www.taxheal.com/notification-of-1-construction-cess-levy-under-the-code-on-social-security-2020.html).
- Owner and contractor can both be liable for the cess; pure supply contracts are exempt — [SupremeToday](https://supremetoday.ai/issue/bocw-cess-liability-contractor-employer-obligations). A BHEL Jan 2026 tender corrigendum makes civil contractors bear the cess within their quoted price — [BHEL](https://tenders.bhel.com/sites/default/files/Corrigendum-IX-dtd-13-01-2026-Technical-2026-01-13-03%3A32%3A17.pdf).
- E-way bill: GSTN advisory of 20/21 May 2026 proposed a mandatory Ship-To GSTIN for bill-to/ship-to and voluntary closure. Put on hold by advisory of 29 Jul 2026; no new date as of 10 Aug 2026. 2FA, 180-day and 360-day limits continue — [busy.in](https://busy.in/business-updates/e-way-bill-changes-2026-gstn-puts-august-updates-on-hold/); [KSandK](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/e-way-bill-portal-enhancements/); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/e-way-bill-changes-june-2026); [taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-news/22-05-2026-mandatory-reporting-of-the-ship-to-gstin-wherever-the-transaction-involves-a-bill-to-ship-to-model-e-way-bill-portal-update/).
- IMS: secondary sources say it has been mandatory since 1 Apr 2026. If you take no action, invoices are deemed accepted. Pending items are excluded from GSTR-2B, and GSTR-2B must be regenerated after any action — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/invoice-management-system-ims-explained-reconcile-itc.html); [LegalRaasta](https://www.legalraasta.com/blog/?p=36605); [TheTaxCorp, Sep 2026](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/gst-invoice-management-system-ims-september-2026-credit-note-pending-rules-gstr). Sources disagree on scope, and none cited an official notification.
- RERA: Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act 2026, passed 2 Apr 2026, effective 7 May 2026, replaces some imprisonment with monetary penalties — [LawSathi](https://lawsathi.in/rera-amendment-2026-legal-analysis-of-the-jan-vishwas-act/); [KSandK](https://ksandk.com/md/newsletter/jan-vishwas-amendment-rera/). UP RERA ninth amendment notified 9 Jan 2026 — [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/insights/rera-uttar-pradesh). The "RERA 2.0" claims (70% escrow, third-party audits, 5-year defect liability) are vendor content with no notification cited — [BeyondWalls](https://corporate.beyondwalls.com/rera-2-0-india-2026-homebuyer-protection).

### Inferences
- "BOCW cess 1% labour code / construction cess self-assessment 2026" is new, specific and only lightly covered (Taxmann, Taxheal). A cess calculator plus a "who pays: contractor or owner" explainer is ownable and fits TSS costing and billing.
- "E-way bill for construction site material / bill-to-ship-to for site delivery" fits TSS materials. The rule is on hold, so content must be framed as "current rules + what is pending".
- "IMS for contractors: accept/reject/pending for supplier and subcontractor invoices" is a good fit (ITC on materials). Hedge the "mandatory" claim.
- RERA is a weak fit for a contractor billing SaaS. Deprioritise.

### Gaps
- TDS on GST (s.51, 2% for government contracts): no 2025–26 change found in this research. Not researched in depth.
- The 8 May 2026 BOCW cess Gazette notification and an official IMS mandate were not read directly.
