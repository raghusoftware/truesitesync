Construction cost overrun: 10 causes and how to control them

Cost overruns rarely come from one disaster. They build up from small leaks nobody measures each week: wastage, idle labour, unbilled extra work, late bills. Here is where they come from and a weekly routine to catch them early.

The scale of it. MoSPI's July 2026 project monitoring data reported cost overruns of about ₹3.4 trillion across central infrastructure projects. For a contractor, overrun means profit lost. The cure is to compare cost against work done every week, not against the payments made.

Early warning signs to watch weekly

📊

Cost % > progress %

Spent 40% of the budget for 30% of the work.

🧱

Consumption over theory

Cement or steel used beyond the allowed wastage.

👷

Output per man-day falling

Same gang, less work, for three days running.

🧾

Work done but not billed

Measured quantity well ahead of billed quantity.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main causes of cost overrun in construction?

Under-estimated quantities, material wastage and price rises, idle labour, rework from wrong drawings, unbilled extra items, delays, idle hired machinery, late billing and penalties.

How can contractors control construction costs?

Compare cost against work done every week, item by item against the BOQ rate. Record every delivery and expense against a site, and bill on a fixed date.

What is the difference between cost overrun and time overrun?

Cost overrun is spending more than the budget; time overrun is finishing later than planned. Time overrun usually causes cost overrun through extended overheads, hire charges and penalties.

How do I know early if a project is going over budget?

Watch four signs weekly: cost % running ahead of progress %, consumption above theoretical quantities, output per man-day falling, and measured work not yet billed.
