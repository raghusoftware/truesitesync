The essentials, in plain English

For a construction contractor, GST comes down to four questions on every bill: what SAC, what rate, which tax split, and on what value. Works-contract services are classified under SAC heading 9954; the headline rate for most works contracts is 18%, though specified categories (certain government, infrastructure or affordable-housing works) can be 12% or concessional. Whether you charge CGST+SGST or IGST depends on whether the supply is intra-state or inter-state — and for construction the place of supply is generally the site's location. The tax is computed on the taxable value of the running-account bill. Because rates and conditions do change, treat this as orientation and confirm specifics with your CA.

Not tax advice: GST rates, SAC sub-codes and RCM conditions are updated periodically. Verify the current position for your contract with a qualified professional.

RCM, ITC and a few traps

A few things catch contractors out. Reverse charge (RCM) can apply to specific supplies (for example certain services or purchases from unregistered suppliers under defined conditions) — where it applies, the recipient pays the GST. Input tax credit (ITC) on works contracts is restricted in some situations (notably where it relates to construction of immovable property on one's own account). And on RA bills, GST is on the taxable value — so retention, advance recovery and TDS interact with the base value in a specific order. Getting that order wrong is a common reconciliation headache, which is why applying it in software beats a spreadsheet.

Frequently asked questions

What SAC code for works contracts?

Generally SAC 9954, with sub-codes by work type — confirm with your CA.

What GST rate applies?

18% for most works contracts; 12%/concessional for specified categories. Verify current rates.

CGST/SGST or IGST?

CGST+SGST intra-state; IGST inter-state (place of supply = site for immovable property).

How is GST shown on an RA bill?
