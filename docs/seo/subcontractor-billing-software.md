# Subcontractor Billing Software: Certify, Deduct and Pay Every Running Bill Right

Every subcontractor bill is a claim. Your job is to pay for the work that was measured, at the rate that was agreed, less everything you're supposed to hold back. **Subcontractor billing software** makes that the default, not something an accountant has to remember.

> **What is subcontractor billing software?**
> Subcontractor billing software lets a main contractor raise and certify subcontractors' running bills against measured quantity and the agreed rate list. It applies retention, TDS and advance recovery consistently and keeps a running ledger per subcontractor. Every payment then matches the work actually executed on site.

## What does subcontractor billing software do?

It puts four things in one record that usually live in four places:

- **The contract:** each subcontractor's work order and agreed rate list
- **The work:** quantity measured on site against those items
- **The bill:** up-to-date quantity, previous bills and the current payable difference
- **The money:** retention held, TDS deducted, advances recovered and net paid

When these are linked, a subcontractor can't be paid for more than was measured, and a deduction can't be skipped because someone was in a hurry on payment day.

## From gross bill to net payable: the deductions that go wrong

A running bill to a subcontractor typically works like this:

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Up-to-date value** | Total measured quantity to date × agreed rate |
| − **Previously certified** | Everything paid in earlier running bills |
| = **This bill (gross)** | Value of work done since the last bill |
| − **Retention** | The percentage held back under the work order |
| − **TDS** | Tax deducted at source on contractor payments (Section 194C) |
| − **Advance recovery** | Mobilisation or cash advances being recovered |
| = **Net payable** | What actually goes to the subcontractor |

*Illustrative structure. Your work order decides which deductions apply.*

Each line is a chance to overpay. Missing "previously certified" means double payment. Forgetting retention leaves you nothing to hold back for defects. An untracked mid-month cash advance means paying it twice. The [Section 194C guide](/blog/section-194c-tds-on-contractor-payments/) explains how TDS sits alongside retention and advance recovery on each running bill.

## How True Site Sync handles subcontractor billing

### Work orders and agreed rate lists

Set up each subcontract as a work order with its own BOQ items, rates, GST treatment and retention percentage. A project can hold several work orders, so civil, MEP and finishing subs are each billed under their own terms. (Not sure how a work order differs from a BOQ? See [work order vs BOQ](/solutions/work-order-vs-boq/).)

### Bill from measured quantity, not claims

Site measurements are recorded in the [digital measurement book](/solutions/measurement-book-software/) against work-order items. They flow into an abstract and a running bill showing up-to-date quantity, previous-bill quantity and the current payable difference. Rates come from the work order, so nobody retypes them.

### Retention, TDS and advances applied every time

Retention is deducted at the percentage set on the work order. GST is applied as CGST/SGST or IGST per work order. TDS and advances are handled in the same billing and payments workflow, so every bill goes from gross to net the same way.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is subcontractor billing?
Subcontractor billing is how a main contractor pays its subcontractors for work done, usually through running account (RA) bills. Each bill values the measured quantity at the agreed rate, subtracts earlier bills, and deducts retention, TDS and any advance recovery to arrive at the net payable.

### How do you prepare a subcontractor RA bill?
Measure the work done against the subcontractor's work-order items, value it at the agreed rates, subtract previously certified amounts, then apply retention, TDS and advance recovery. Our step-by-step [RA bill guide](/blog/how-to-prepare-ra-bill-india/) walks through the format.

### Is TDS deducted on subcontractor bills?
Yes. Payments to contractors and subcontractors for carrying out work generally attract TDS under Section 194C (Section 393 from FY 2026-27). The deduction comes off each running bill along with retention and advance recovery. Check current rates and thresholds with your CA.

### Can I track retention for each subcontractor?
Yes. Retention is set as a percentage on each work order and deducted automatically on every running bill, so the amount held back is always visible per subcontractor.

### Can one project have several subcontractors?
Yes. A project can carry multiple work orders, each with its own rates, GST and retention, so each subcontractor is measured and billed separately within the same project.

### Does it work for labour contractors as well?
Yes. Labour contractors can track gang attendance and convert measured work into bills, so daily-wage and piece-rate settlements sit in the same system as your other subcontractors.
