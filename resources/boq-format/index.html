What is a BOQ?

A BOQ (Bill of Quantities) is a document that lists every item of work in a project with its description, unit of measurement, quantity and rate. It's used to price tenders, compare bids, and later measure and bill the work through RA bills.

How to prepare a BOQ in 6 steps

1. Study the GFC drawings

Architectural, structural and MEP, plus the specification.

2. List items by trade

Earthwork, PCC, RCC, steel, shuttering, masonry, plaster, flooring, finishes, MEP.

3. Take off quantities

Measure as per IS 1200, with deductions for openings. Use the concrete, brick and BBS calculators.

4. Write full descriptions

Material, grade, mix, thickness and finish: a description should price the item on its own.

5. Apply rates

From the state SOR / DSR for government work, or rate analysis and market quotes for private work.

6. Total and add GST

Amount = quantity × rate; add GST as per the contract (18% for most works contracts).

Common BOQ mistakes

  • Vague descriptions ("RCC work") that can't be priced or measured.
  • Mixing units for the same item (m² vs m³ for plaster).
  • Missing items, which become disputed extra items later.
  • Heading rows and blank rows inside the item list, which break imports and totals.
  • Not tracking BOQ vs actual quantities during the job. See BOQ vs actual cost.

Frequently asked questions

What is the format of a BOQ?

A table with Item No, Description, Unit, Quantity, Rate and Amount, where Amount = Quantity × Rate, grouped by trade, with a total and GST at the end.

How do I prepare a BOQ for building construction?

Study the drawings and specification, list items by trade, take off quantities as per IS 1200, write full descriptions, apply SOR or market rates, then total and add GST.

What is the difference between BOQ and estimate?

An estimate is the owner's or contractor's forecast of cost. A BOQ is the contract document listing quantities that bidders price and against which the work is measured and billed.
