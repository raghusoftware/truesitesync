# Vendor blogs own five winnable Indian SERPs

Across all five target keywords (BOQ vs actual cost software, subcontractor billing software India, labour attendance software for construction sites, construction software pricing India, and construction petty cash management), the results that surface are almost entirely **vendor-written content**: competitor blogs and feature pages (Onsite, Powerplay, SiteSetu, Quantbit BuildX, biCanvas, Shivit, Truein, Toco), self-ranking "Top 10 in India 2026" listicles (constructionestimatorindia.com, biCanvas, Petpooja, Aasaan), directories (Capterra India, Techjockey) and, for some queries, off-intent pages such as job ads, MGNREGA documents or US accounting guides. AI answer engines therefore build their recommendations from those same listicles, and the search engine's own AI summaries repeatedly hedged that sources were "vendor marketing", "conflicting" or lacking any head-to-head comparison. No ranking page pairs a construction-specific worked example in rupees with correct, dated Indian law and a neutral, sourced comparison table. That combination is the opening for True Site Sync. Correctness is also a differentiator, because several competitors and **True Site Sync's own live pages** carry outdated facts: the ₹4.28 lakh crore MoSPI overrun figure dates from 2021, Section 194C was renumbered as Section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 on 1 April 2026, works-contract GST moved generally to 18% on 22 September 2025, and the four Labour Codes took effect on 21 November 2025. One caveat governs everything below. The research ran on a US-located search tool with no rank positions, no People Also Ask boxes and no Google AI Overview capture, and every direct page fetch was blocked. "Ranking" therefore means "surfaced by the search index". Each claim about competitors comes from search snippets, not full page reads, and must be checked manually before publishing.

## AI engines cite listicles because nothing neutral exists

The search tool's synthesised answers, the only AI output observed, behave the same way across all five keywords: they draw recommendations from vendor listicles and flag the weakness of those sources. For "best subcontractor billing software in India", the answer cited aiaccountant.com, realxerp.com, three constructionestimatorindia.com pages, buildbite.com, bicanvas.com, nwayerp.com and concorderp.com. It labelled them "vendor-published roundups" and closed with "pilot and verify GST/TDS features" ([AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/construction-accounting-software); [Nway](https://www.nwayerp.com/best-construction-management-software-india-2026/); [Concord](https://concorderp.com/blogs/best-construction-management-software-in-india-2026/)). For BOQ software, it named Realx Estimation, BuildCost and BuildNext with CEI prices and noted that pricing was inconsistent and no head-to-head comparison existed ([CEI](https://constructionestimatorindia.com/?p=5729)). For labour attendance, it recommended HajariBook, Powerplay and Truein, adding that results were "mostly vendor pages" ([HajariBook](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1661537437); [Powerplay](https://www.getpowerplay.in/labour-management/); [Truein](https://truein.com/contract-workers-attendance-old)). For petty cash, it said outright that it "found no source on the specific mechanics of settling a site engineer's advance" and that none gave "an Indian voucher template, GST or TDS treatment for site expenses". The search tool reported no Reddit or Quora threads for these India queries, while LinkedIn posts did appear in the citation pool ([LinkedIn](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/nimerikhalifa)).

Three conclusions follow. First, the pages most likely to be lifted into AI answers are those with **short definitional sentences, formulas stated verbatim, ₹ tables, and dated facts with official links**. Shivit's formula is already quoted this way. Second, because engines openly distrust the current sources, a comparison table that discloses who publishes it, links every competitor price to its source and carries a "last verified" date has a real chance of displacing the listicles. Third, AI engines also pull from third-party roundups, so True Site Sync should seek inclusion on the pages already cited (constructionestimatorindia.com, aiaccountant.com, buildbite.com, Capterra India) as well as publish its own pages. One counterpoint is that the observed "AI answer" is a search-engine synthesis, not Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini, which could weight sources differently. Treat this as directional until tested from an Indian IP.

### BOQ vs actual cost software

**H1:** "BOQ vs Actual Cost: Formula, Variance Report and Software (India)". **H2s:** answer-first definition; BOQ vs budget vs committed vs actual; the formulas (stated verbatim, with both sign conventions); quantity vs rate variance worked example; comparing at partial completion (EAC and cost-to-complete); the data flow BOQ → indent → PO → GRN → MB → RA bill → variance; free BOQ variance template and calculator; Excel vs software; how True Site Sync does it; why it matters (MoSPI July 2026 figure, scoped to central projects); FAQ. The **formula block**: Quantity variance = (Actual qty − BOQ qty) × BOQ rate; Rate variance = (Actual rate − BOQ rate) × Actual qty; QV + RV = Actual cost − BOQ cost; CPI = EV ÷ AC; EAC = BAC ÷ CPI; cost to complete = EAC − AC. The **worked example**: M25 concrete with BOQ 100 m³ at ₹6,000 = ₹6,00,000, actual 108 m³ at ₹6,300 = ₹6,80,400, giving QV ₹48,000 + RV ₹32,400 = ₹80,400 overrun. Fe500 steel with BOQ 20 t at ₹65,000 = ₹13,00,000, actual 19.5 t at ₹68,000 = ₹13,26,000, giving QV −₹32,500 + RV ₹58,500 = ₹26,000. This shows a quantity saving hidden by a price rise. **Partial completion**: BAC ₹5.00 crore, 40% measured complete so EV ₹2.00 crore, AC ₹2.20 crore; CV −₹20 lakh, CPI 0.91, EAC ≈ ₹5.50 crore, cost to complete ≈ ₹3.30 crore. **FAQs**: what is BOQ vs actual cost; how to calculate cost variance; quantity vs rate variance; committed vs actual cost; what a BOQ variance report contains; can I do it in Excel; BOQ vs BOM; how RA bills and the MB link to the BOQ. **Schema**: FAQPage, HowTo ("how to compare BOQ and actual cost"), SoftwareApplication, BreadcrumbList. The existing `/solutions/boq-management-software/` page should link to this page as the variance deep-dive.

### Subcontractor billing software India

**H1:** "Subcontractor Billing Software for Indian Contractors (RA Bills, TDS, GST)". **H2s:** what a subcontractor RA bill is; work-order clauses to fix before the first bill; worked sub-bill; the 2026 legal box (Section 393 / formerly 194C, GST 2.0, s.51 GST TDS for government work); back-to-back billing to the client RA bill and margin per BOQ item; thekedar and labour-contractor bills (Hinglish section); MEP subcontracts (JMR, material reconciliation); Excel/Tally vs Onsite/Powerplay/Quantbit/biCanvas vs True Site Sync on the criteria AI answers use; downloadable format; FAQ. **Worked sub-bill**: cumulative measured value ₹10,00,000 less previously certified ₹6,00,000 = this bill ₹4,00,000. Add GST 18% ₹72,000. ## Conclusion

The competitive picture is less about out-writing rivals than about being the first page in each SERP that is both **specific and verifiably correct**. The incumbents rank because they exist, not because they answer. AI engines are already signalling distrust of their sources, and several incumbents, including True Site Sync's own older pages, carry facts that the April 2026 Income-tax Act, GST 2.0 and the Labour Codes made obsolete. A page that states formulas and law in quotable sentences, shows the arithmetic in rupees, and dates every source can move from absent to cited faster than backlinks alone would predict. The pricing evidence also hands True Site Sync an unusually strong comparison story, at about ₹1,000 per user per year against Onsite's ₹12,000, provided it is presented as dated list-price arithmetic with a disclosure.

The sequencing implication is clear. Correct the existing pages first, verify the live SERPs and legal texts on the checklist, and only then publish the five pages, starting with petty cash and subcontractor billing, where the gaps are widest and the product already has the features to back the claims. Pursuing inclusion in the third-party listicles that AI engines already cite should run in parallel. Owning the citation pool is likely to matter as much as owning the ranking.
